Before you begin

What is this, really?

Imagine a librarian for mutual funds

WealthTicker keeps a big, up-to-date library of Indian mutual funds. You can ask it questions like “show me Nifty index funds” or “tell me everything about this one UTI fund,” and it answers with clean, structured facts — returns, fees, risk, and more.

  • You ask by visiting a web address (called an endpoint) with your private access key.
  • It answers with facts pulled from public AMFI NAV data and WealthTicker’s own calculations.
  • This guide shows live answers we received, rewritten so a non-technical reader can understand them.

Your access key stays private

Every request needs a personal key (created under Settings → API keys on WealthTicker). This guide intentionally does not store or display your key.

Free plan limits: about 100 asks per day / 2,000 per month. NAV history on free is roughly the trailing 1 year.

What we covered

Search funds, one-fund details, NAV history, all categories, all fund houses, and one fund-house profile.

What we skipped

Two Pro-only asks: portfolio overlap between funds, and full stock-holding lists. Those need a Pro plan.

Base address

https://wealthticker.in/api/v1 — every ask below starts from here.

Optional filters you can add to the ask

  • category / subCategory — Equity, Debt, Index Funds, and so on
  • amc — only one fund house (e.g. HDFC)
  • plan — Direct or Regular
  • option — Growth or IDCW (dividend-style payout)
  • minAum / maxExpenseRatio — “big enough funds” / “cheap enough fees”
  • sort + dir — order by a field ascending or descending

Word guide

Jargon → everyday meaning

NAV

The price of one unit of the fund for that day — like the share price of the fund.

AUM

How much money investors have parked in the fund. Bigger isn’t always better, but it shows scale.

Expense ratio

The yearly fee the fund charges, shown as a percentage of your investment.

CAGR

The smoothed yearly growth rate over multiple years (not a simple total).

Direct vs Regular

Direct plans skip distributor commissions and usually have lower fees than Regular plans.

Growth vs IDCW

Growth reinvests profits into the fund. IDCW can pay out money from time to time.

Volatility

How much the fund’s value tends to swing up and down.

Max drawdown

The worst fall from a peak to a low — a gut-check for “how bad can it get?”

Sharpe / Sortino

Scores for “return per unit of risk.” Higher usually means a smoother ride for the return earned.

Scheme code

The unique ID for a fund. Use it when you want details or NAV history for exactly one scheme.

Slug

A short web-friendly name for a fund house (example: hdfc).

Pro endpoints

Overlap and portfolio-stats need a paid Pro plan — not covered in this free guide.

Important reminder

This guide explains data. It is not investment advice. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks. Always read the scheme documents and verify numbers before making decisions.
